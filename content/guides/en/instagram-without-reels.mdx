Instagram is where a lot of everyday social life happens: DMs with friends, close-friends Stories, group chats, the occasional photo dump. Reels is where the time disappears. If you want one without the other, you have more options than you might think — but they're not equal, and a few come with real risks.

Here's every approach we know of, compared honestly.

## The quick comparison

| Option | Removes Reels? | Keeps DMs & Stories? | Effort | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "Not interested" & snoozing | No | Yes | Low | None |
| Daily time reminder | No | Yes | Low | None |
| Using Instagram in a browser | Partly | Mostly | Medium | None |
| Old app versions / modded apps | Sometimes | Yes | High | **High** |
| Android app timers | No | Limits them too | Low | None |
| Blocking Reels screens (e.g. Reelless) | Yes | Yes | Low | Permission to review |
| Deleting Instagram | Yes | No | Low | Social cost |

Now the details.

## 1. Teach the algorithm: "Not interested" and snoozing

On any reel, the menu offers **Not interested**. In your feed, you can **snooze suggested posts** for a period of time.

**Pros:** Built-in, free, no risk.
**Cons:** It changes *which* reels you see, not *how many*. The Reels tab, the Reels player and swipe-to-next all remain. Snoozes expire.

**Verdict:** Worth doing, but it won't change your habits by itself.

## 2. Instagram's daily time reminder

In *Your activity → Time spent*, you can set a daily reminder.

**Pros:** Gives you awareness at the moment it matters.
**Cons:** One tap dismisses it. It also counts all Instagram time, so replying to messages uses up the same budget as watching Reels.

**Verdict:** Useful for awareness, weak as a barrier.

## 3. Use Instagram in a mobile browser

Uninstall the app and log in through your phone's browser.

**Pros:** The website is less polished than the app, and that friction reduces mindless use. Some features that drive endless watching behave differently on the web.
**Cons:** Reels still exist on the website. DMs work but are clunkier, and posting can be awkward. Notifications are different, so you may miss messages.

**Verdict:** A reasonable choice if you mainly use Instagram on a computer.

## 4. Old app versions and modified apps

You'll find advice online to install an older version of Instagram from before Reels existed, or a modified third-party app that strips Reels out.

**Pros:** They can genuinely remove Reels.
**Cons:** This is where we'd urge caution. Modified apps ask for your Instagram **username and password**, which puts your account at real risk. They may violate Instagram's terms, which can lead to your account being restricted. Old versions miss security fixes, and Instagram may stop them from working at any time.

**Verdict:** Not recommended. The trade-off isn't worth your account security.

## 5. Android app timers

Digital Wellbeing (or your phone maker's equivalent) can cap Instagram at, say, 30 minutes a day.

**Pros:** Free, simple, built into Android.
**Cons:** The timer can't tell Reels from DMs. If you spend your budget on Reels, you're locked out of messages too — which often leads to people turning the timer off.

**Verdict:** Fine if all of your Instagram time is the problem. Frustrating if only Reels is.

## 6. Block Reels screens on Android

Apps that use Android's **Accessibility Service** can recognize when a Reels screen opens inside Instagram and close it. This is what [Reelless does on Instagram](/block-instagram-reels):

- The **Reels player** closes as soon as it appears.
- The **Reels tab** does nothing when tapped.
- The **Explore grid** (mostly reels) is covered by default.
- **One and done** lets you watch a reel a friend sends you — but not the next one.

DMs, Stories, your feed and posting keep working.

**Pros:** Removes Reels specifically, without touching your account. Reelless never asks for your Instagram password and works entirely on your device.
**Cons:** Requires the Accessibility permission, which is sensitive. Read how any app uses it before granting it — ours is described in the [Accessibility Service disclosure](/accessibility-disclosure). Android only.

**Verdict:** The closest thing to "Instagram without Reels" that doesn't risk your account.

## 7. Delete Instagram

**Pros:** Completely solves the problem. Free.
**Cons:** You lose DMs, Stories and the social connections that live there. For many people — especially those whose friends coordinate everything on Instagram — that's a real cost.

**Verdict:** Right for some people. If you can do it without losing anything that matters to you, it's the simplest option of all.

## What about iPhone?

On iPhone, no app can act inside Instagram, so option 6 isn't possible. The best native combination is: snooze suggestions, set Screen Time limits, keep Instagram off your home screen, and consider using it in Safari.

## Habits that help, whatever you choose

- **Turn off notifications** for likes, suggested content and live videos. Keep only DMs.
- **Move Instagram off your home screen** into a folder.
- **Decide when you check messages** — after lunch, after work — rather than whenever.
- **Notice your triggers.** Most reel sessions start from boredom, tiredness, or avoiding something. A replacement activity (a book, a walk, a podcast) works better than willpower.

## A note on the reel a friend sends you

The hardest case isn't the Reels tab — it's the reel that arrives in a DM. You want to see it, because it's part of a conversation, but the moment it ends the next one is a swipe away. Whatever approach you choose, it helps to have a rule for this. Some people watch shared reels only on a computer. Others reply first and watch later. On Android, Reelless's **One and done** lets the shared reel play and closes the player when you swipe for the next — so you stay in the conversation without falling into the feed behind it.

## Our recommendation

Start with what's free and safe: snooze suggestions, trim notifications, move the icon. If Reels still eats your evenings and you're on Android, block Reels screens specifically — it keeps the social part of Instagram intact. Avoid modified apps that ask for your password. And if you realize you don't miss Instagram at all once Reels is gone, that's worth knowing too.
