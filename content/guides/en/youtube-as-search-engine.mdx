YouTube is arguably the most useful search engine you have that isn't Google. Need to change a bike tire, understand a statistics concept, learn a chord, or fix a dishwasher? Someone has made a clear video about it.

The trouble is that YouTube doesn't want to be a search engine. It wants to be a feed. Open the app and you get a grid of videos chosen to be clicked; finish a video and the next one starts automatically. You came for a tire, and you leave an hour later with a flat tire and opinions about a documentary on deep-sea fish.

This guide is about flipping that: making search the default, and the feed something you visit only on purpose.

## The idea: pull, not push

There are two ways to get content:

- **Pull:** You have a question, you search, you watch the answer, you leave.
- **Push:** Content is chosen for you and presented before you've asked for anything.

Pull is intentional. Push is where time disappears. The home feed, "Up next", autoplay, end screens and Shorts are all push. Search, subscriptions and playlists are mostly pull. Every change below moves you from push toward pull.

## Step 1: Turn off autoplay

In YouTube's settings (and in the player itself, via the autoplay toggle), turn **autoplay off**.

**What it changes:** When a video ends, the next one doesn't start on its own. You get a natural stopping cue — the moment to decide whether you're done.

**Why it matters:** Autoplay turns "do nothing" into "keep watching". Turning it off restores a decision you didn't know you'd lost. We go deeper into why defaults matter in [why short videos are so hard to stop](/guides/why-short-videos-are-hard-to-stop).

## Step 2: Start from search, not Home

Make a habit of opening YouTube straight into search:

- Many Android launchers let you long-press the YouTube icon for a **Search** shortcut you can pin to your home screen.
- In a browser, bookmark the YouTube search results page rather than the homepage.
- Or simply tap search the instant the app opens, before your eyes land on the grid.

**Why it matters:** The first thing you see shapes what you do. If the first thing is a search bar, you start with your own question.

## Step 3: Pause watch history (if it suits you)

In your Google account's YouTube history settings, you can **pause watch history**. At the time of writing, if your history is paused and you have no previous history, YouTube may show a prompt instead of a personalized home feed.

**Pros:** The home feed largely disappears without any extra tools.
**Cons:** Recommendations become less relevant everywhere, "continue watching" disappears, and if you resume history the feed comes back. Not everyone will like the trade-off.

Try it for a week and see how it feels.

## Step 4: Make Subscriptions your home

Subscriptions are a middle ground: content is pushed to you, but only from creators you explicitly chose. Prune them regularly — unsubscribe from channels you watch out of habit rather than interest — and use the Subscriptions tab as your starting point instead of Home.

## Step 5: Use playlists and Watch Later as a queue

When something interesting shows up — in a search, from a friend, in your subscriptions — don't watch it now. Save it to **Watch Later** or a playlist. Then set aside a specific time to watch your queue.

**Why it matters:** It separates *finding* from *watching*. You still get to watch great videos; you just choose when.

## Step 6: Handle Shorts separately

Shorts is the purest push surface on YouTube, and none of the steps above remove it. Your options are covered in detail in [How to stop watching YouTube Shorts without deleting YouTube](/guides/stop-watching-youtube-shorts). In short: you can hide the shelf temporarily from the home feed, but on Android you need a tool if you want the Shorts player gone for good.

## Step 7 (optional): Enforce search-only

Habits are great until you're tired. If you want YouTube to *stay* a search engine even on bad days, you can enforce it.

On Android, [Reelless Search-only mode](/youtube-without-recommendations) does this:

- The **home feed is covered** with a calm screen and a search bar.
- **"Up next" and end-screen suggestions are covered**, so finishing a video doesn't lead to another.
- **Shorts stays blocked.**
- **Subscriptions** can stay visible or be hidden — your choice.

You can also put it on a **schedule** — search-only during work or study hours, normal YouTube in the evening — and add **Strict mode** so you can't quietly switch it off when a deadline gets uncomfortable.

The trade-off: it needs Android's Accessibility permission to recognize those screens. Reelless processes everything on your device and collects no screen content; read the [Accessibility Service disclosure](/accessibility-disclosure) before deciding.

## A setup that works for different people

**For students:** Autoplay off, Search-only mode during study hours, a "Lectures" playlist as your queue. Normal YouTube after your work is done.

**For professionals:** Start from search, prune subscriptions to the handful of channels that help your work, Watch Later for everything else.

**For families:** Search-only on a shared tablet makes YouTube a tool children use to look things up, rather than a feed that decides what they watch next.

**For anyone:** Autoplay off and starting from search — two changes, five minutes — already make a noticeable difference.

## Common objections

**"But I discover great videos through recommendations."** You do — and you can keep that. Schedule discovery instead of letting it happen all day: browse your Subscriptions or Home on purpose for twenty minutes on a weekend, save what looks good, and leave.

**"Search results show recommendations too."** They can. That's why the queue habit matters: save interesting results instead of following them immediately.

**"This feels like a lot of effort."** Start with two changes — autoplay off and starting from search. Add more only if you need them.

## What you keep

None of this removes what makes YouTube valuable. You still get the tutorial, the lecture, the recipe and the music video. You just get them when you ask, and then you get your time back.
