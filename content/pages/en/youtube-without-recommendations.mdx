## Why the home feed is the real trap

You open YouTube to look up how to fix a leaking tap. The home screen shows you twenty videos chosen because people like you clicked on them. Forty minutes later, the tap is still leaking.

The home feed and the "Up next" column aren't neutral — they're built to maximize watch time, and they're very good at it. **Search**, on the other hand, starts with *your* question. Search-only mode keeps that part and removes the rest.

## What YouTube offers on its own

YouTube doesn't have a "search only" setting, but there are a couple of partial workarounds, which we cover in depth in [Using YouTube as a search engine, not a feed](/guides/youtube-as-search-engine):

- **Pause watch history.** At the time of writing, if your history is off and you have no prior history, YouTube may replace the home feed with a prompt. It's a clever trick with side effects: recommendations get worse everywhere, and resuming history brings the feed back.
- **Turn off autoplay.** This stops the next video starting automatically, but the suggestions are still right there.
- **Use subscriptions as your home.** Helpful as a habit, not enforced by the app.

## What Search-only mode does

When Search-only mode is on, Reelless reshapes YouTube on your phone:

- **The home feed is covered.** You see a calm screen with a search bar instead of a grid of thumbnails.
- **Recommendations under and beside videos are covered**, including the end-screen suggestions that pull you into the next one.
- **Shorts stay blocked** — player, shelves and tab.
- **Subscriptions are your choice.** Keep them for creators you deliberately follow, or hide them for a stricter setup.

What keeps working: **search, the video you searched for, comments, playlists, your library and downloads.**

## How to set it up

1. Install Reelless and choose **YouTube**.
2. Turn on **Search-only mode** in YouTube's settings inside Reelless.
3. Decide whether to keep **Subscriptions** visible.
4. Allow Reelless in Android's **Accessibility settings** when prompted. The app explains the permission first, and so does our [How it works](/how-it-works) page.
5. Open YouTube. You should land on a search bar.

## Make it fit your day

Search-only mode doesn't have to be permanent. With **schedules** you can turn it on automatically during work or study hours and off in the evening. With **Strict mode**, changing the schedule requires a waiting period, so you can't quietly switch it off when a deadline gets uncomfortable.

## Who it's for

- **Students** who use YouTube for lectures and tutorials, but lose study time to recommended videos.
- **Professionals** who need YouTube for work — software walkthroughs, talks, documentation — without the detours.
- **Parents** who want YouTube on a family device to be a search tool rather than an endless feed.
- **Anyone** who has ever opened YouTube for one thing and forgotten what it was.

## Private by design

Search-only mode runs entirely on your phone. Reelless doesn't sign in to your Google account, doesn't see your watch history, and doesn't send anything about your screen anywhere. Learn more in the [Accessibility Service disclosure](/accessibility-disclosure).
