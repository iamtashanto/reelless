## Facebook, minus the reel loop

Facebook has changed. A feed that used to be mostly your friends now mixes in Reels rows, suggested videos, and a Video tab built around short clips. For people who still rely on Facebook for family updates, school groups, local buy-and-sell or event invites, that's frustrating: you can't leave, but you can't open it without getting pulled into a reel.

Reelless lets you keep Facebook as a tool for staying in touch, and stops the short-video part from taking over.

## What Facebook allows today

At the time of writing, Facebook offers a few partial controls:

- **Hide reel / Show less** on individual reels. This tunes recommendations rather than removing Reels.
- **The Feeds tab** with filters like *Friends* or *Groups*. It's genuinely useful, but it's a separate view — the default home feed still contains Reels.
- **Snooze** a person, Page or group for 30 days. Helpful for specific sources, not for Reels as a format.
- **Time reminders** under *Your time on Facebook*, which nudge rather than stop.

None of these turn Reels off. We cover each in more depth in [How to turn off Reels on Facebook](/guides/turn-off-facebook-reels).

## What Reelless blocks on Facebook

- **The Reels player** closes automatically, wherever it opened from.
- **Reels rows in News Feed** are covered with a calm panel so you can scroll past them.
- **The Video / Reels tab** won't open.
- **Suggested posts and reels** are hidden with **Friends-first** mode, if you turn it on. See [Facebook without suggested posts](/facebook-without-suggested-posts) for how that works.

Everything else keeps working: friends' posts, groups, events, Marketplace, Pages you follow, notifications, and links that open Messenger.

## Facebook Lite works too

Facebook Lite is popular on budget phones and slow connections — and it has Reels as well. Reelless supports Facebook Lite alongside the main app, with the same controls. If you use both, protect both.

## How to set it up

1. Install Reelless and choose **Facebook** (and **Facebook Lite**, if you use it).
2. Keep **Reels player**, **Reels rows** and **Reels tab** blocked. Turn on **Friends-first** if suggested posts bother you too.
3. Enable Reelless in Android's **Accessibility settings** when asked. Reelless explains exactly what the permission is for before you do; you can read the same explanation on [How it works](/how-it-works).
4. Open Facebook and scroll your feed. Reels rows should appear covered, and tapping a reel should drop you straight back.

## Gentle limits, not just blocks

If you're not ready to cut Reels completely, set a **daily short-form allowance** — five or ten minutes a day — and Reelless will close the player once it's used up. You can also set a **pause before open**: a few seconds of breathing before Facebook launches, so opening it is a choice rather than a reflex.

## What Reelless doesn't do

Reelless doesn't log in to your account, read your messages, post anything, or change your Facebook settings. It also doesn't block ads — it isn't an ad blocker. Recognition happens on your phone, and no screen content is collected or sent. Full details are in our [Accessibility Service disclosure](/accessibility-disclosure).
