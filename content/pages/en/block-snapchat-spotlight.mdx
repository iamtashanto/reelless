## Spotlight is a different app hiding inside Snapchat

Snapchat is, at its heart, a messaging app: Snaps, chats, streaks, Stories from friends. But one tab away sits **Spotlight**, a full-screen feed of short videos from strangers, chosen to keep you swiping. It's easy to open Snapchat to answer a friend and come back up for air half an hour later.

Reelless lets you keep Snapchat for what you actually use it for.

## What Snapchat offers

At the time of writing, Snapchat's own controls are limited when it comes to Spotlight:

- You can **long-press a Spotlight video** to hide it or report it, which tunes recommendations.
- **Family Center** lets parents see who a teen is talking to, but doesn't remove Spotlight.
- There's **no setting to remove the Spotlight tab** or limit time spent in it.

So the usual advice comes down to willpower — which is exactly the thing an endless feed is designed to wear down.

## What Reelless blocks on Snapchat

- **Spotlight.** Open the tab, and Reelless sends you straight back.
- **The Discover stories feed**, optionally, if you find yourself drifting there too.

What keeps working: **Snaps and chat, friends' Stories, the camera, Snap Map, and your streaks.**

## How to set it up

1. Install Reelless and choose **Snapchat**.
2. Spotlight is blocked by default; switch on **Discover** if you want that gone as well.
3. Turn on Reelless in Android's **Accessibility settings** when it asks. It explains what the permission is used for first — you can also read it on [How it works](/how-it-works).
4. Open Snapchat and tap Spotlight. You'll land back where you were.

## Why this matters more than it seems

Messaging apps are where we stay close to people. When a short-video feed lives one tap away from the chat screen, every "quick reply" becomes an opportunity to drift. Taking Spotlight out of the equation keeps Snapchat feeling like what it was meant to be: a way to talk to friends.

If you want to understand *why* these feeds are so hard to stop — it isn't a personal failing — we wrote an explainer: [Doomscrolling: why short videos are so hard to stop](/guides/why-short-videos-are-hard-to-stop).

## Limits that work with you

You don't have to go all-or-nothing:

- A **daily short-form allowance** gives you a few minutes of Spotlight per day.
- **Schedules** can block Spotlight only during study or work hours, or at bedtime.
- **Pause before open** adds a short breathing moment before Snapchat launches.

## Private by design

Reelless doesn't log in to Snapchat, read your chats, or see your Snaps. It recognizes the layout of the Spotlight screen on your phone and presses back — nothing about your screen is stored or sent anywhere.
